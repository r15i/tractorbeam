# Mitigation Strategies against GPS Hijacking

While this project demonstrates the feasibility of a "Tractor Beam" attack via GPS and sensor spoofing, a comprehensive engineering analysis must propose defensive countermeasures. Below are strategies that flight controller firmware developers could implement to mitigate such attacks.

## 1. Sensor Cross-Checking and EKF Strictness
Currently, flight controllers are often tuned to heavily trust GPS once a lock is achieved to prevent flyaways caused by IMU drift.
**Mitigation:** 
- Tighten the EKF variance thresholds. If the integral of the IMU acceleration wildly disagrees with the derivative of the GPS position for more than a few seconds, the FC should trigger a "Sensor Mismatch Failsafe".
- In this failsafe, the drone should ignore GPS entirely and rely on Barometer/IMU to perform a slow, leveled vertical descent, rather than attempting a Return-To-Home (RTH) based on compromised coordinates.

## 2. GPS Signal Authentication
Standard civilian GPS (L1 C/A) is unencrypted and easily generated by Software Defined Radios (SDRs).
**Mitigation:** 
- Transition to modern GNSS receivers capable of **OSNMA (Open Service Navigation Message Authentication)**, a feature of the Galileo satellite network. OSNMA cryptographically signs the navigation data, allowing the receiver to verify that the signal came from a real satellite, not a ground-based spoofer.

## 3. Optical Flow and Odometry Fallbacks
Spoofing both GPS and IMU electronically is possible in a lab (as demonstrated), but spoofing a drone's vision in the real world is significantly harder.
**Mitigation:**
- Integrate **Optical Flow sensors** (downward-facing cameras) that track the ground moving beneath the drone. If the GPS claims the drone is moving at 15 m/s, but the optical flow sensor detects zero lateral movement across the ground, the system can instantly flag the GPS data as spoofed.

## 4. Multi-Receiver Spatial Diversity
A single spoofing transmitter blasts the same signal at the entire drone. Real satellites transmit from vastly different points in space.
**Mitigation:**
- Using two separated GPS antennas on the drone (e.g., one on the front, one on the back). By analyzing the minute timing differences (phase shift) of the incoming signals between the two antennas, the drone can determine the Angle of Arrival. If all satellite signals are arriving from the exact same angle (the attacker's transmitter), the drone can classify it as a spoofing attack.
